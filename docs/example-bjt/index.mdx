## Overview

A CMOS process has no dedicated bipolar steps, but it still gives you a usable
**vertical PNP** for free. The p+ diffusion that normally forms a PMOS source or drain
becomes the emitter, the n-well becomes the base, and the p-type substrate becomes the
collector. Because the collector is the substrate, it is always tied to ground, so the
device is almost always used **diode-connected**, with base and collector both grounded.

This is the device at the heart of bandgap references and on-chip temperature sensors.
The example uses the sky130 `pnp_05v5_W3p40L3p40` device (3.4 µm × 3.4 µm emitter),
biased with a 10 µA current source.

## Theory

With base and collector grounded, the emitter voltage is the emitter–base voltage,
set by the bias current $I$ and the saturation current $I_S$:

$$
V_{EB} = V_T \ln\!\left(\frac{I}{I_S}\right), \qquad V_T = \frac{kT}{q} \tag{1}
$$

$V_{EB}$ falls by roughly 2 mV/K as temperature rises (it is CTAT). The difference
between two such devices run at different current densities, for example one device
against $N$ identical devices in parallel at the same total current, rises with
temperature instead (it is PTAT):

$$
\Delta V_{EB} = V_T \ln N \tag{2}
$$

A bandgap reference adds a scaled copy of (2) to (1) so the two temperature
coefficients cancel.

From the centre outwards:

- **Emitter (E):** p+ diffusion with an array of contacts, strapped in met1.
- **Base (B):** the n-well, contacted through an n+ tap ring just inside the well edge.
- **Collector (C):** the p-substrate, contacted through an outer p+ tap ring.
