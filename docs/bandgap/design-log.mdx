# Bandgap Design Log

This is the full story behind the [bandgap reference](./index.mdx): what I expected at each step,
what the simulator actually said, how I worked out why, and what I changed. It's in the order it happened.
Some of the surprises were circuit problems, some were simulator behaviour, and a few were my own scripting mistakes.
All three kinds cost time, so all three are here.

## At a glance

| # | Unexpected result | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | First design looked fine, but the amplifier tail was in triode | Standard PMOS input pair needs about 2 V of stack | LVT input pair |
| 2 | 3.2 mV amplifier offset | Unequal drain voltages in the amplifier load | Longer devices for more gain |
| 3 | xschem netlist VREF 27 mV higher than the hand netlist | xschem uses a different model library | Standardized on xschem's library, retuned R2 |
| 4 | Temperature sweep gave VREF = −3 kV | DC solver found a nonphysical solution | `.nodeset` guess |
| 5 | PSRR −20 dB, line regulation 107 mV/V | Tail headroom set by VDD; amplifier output referenced to ground | NMOS-input amplifier with PMOS load |
| 6 | Loop gain *rose* to +49 dB at high frequency | Compensation cap broke the injection assumption | Moved the cap to the other side of the probe |
| 7 | Phase margin −87° | My formula reported phase, not margin | Margin = 180° + phase |
| 8 | Fatal model error at the ss corner | 20 µm-long transistor outside the model's valid range | Shortened it to 8 µm |
| 9 | Monte Carlo mean of −26 V, then outliers at 0.68 V and 2.86 V | Script bugs, then DC-solver outliers | Transient power-up per sample |
| 10 | Mismatch σ ≈ 32 mV | Small matched devices | 4× area at the same W/L |
| 11 | VREF exactly 1.200 V costs temperature flatness | First-order bandgap curvature | Chose 1.200 V; documented the trade-off |
| 12 | Tail in triode at 125 °C (margin −61 mV) | Input common-mode falls with $V_{BE}$ | Level-shift resistors plus LVT PMOS |
| 13 | After the fix: nonsense operating point, corners at 1.7 V | More DC solutions; sweep jumped to an unstable one | Fuller nodeset; sweep outward from 27 °C |
| 14 | Mismatch σ jumped to 109 mV | The new resistor pair's mismatch | Wide resistors, 54× the area |
| 15 | Small kink at 27 °C in the temperature plot | Default solver tolerance is 1.2 mV on VREF | `reltol=1e-5` |

The sections below take each one in turn, with the tooling problems at the end.

## Phase 1: sizing with a hand netlist

**The plan.** I chose the classic topology: three matched PMOS mirrors, Q1 (1×), R1 plus Q2 (8×), R2 plus Q3 (1×),
and an error amplifier forcing $V_A = V_B$. I wrote a plain SPICE netlist first, because sizing is faster
there than redrawing a schematic. My starting guesses:

- **Mirrors:** 4/2 µm, aiming for about 5 µA per branch.
- **Amplifier:** a five-transistor OTA with PMOS inputs.
- **R1:** a 2 µm `res_xhigh_po_0p35`.
- **R2:** about ten times R1.
- **Start-up:** a three-transistor circuit.

I also worked out the feedback polarity before simulating. Branch B has R1 in series, so it has the
higher incremental resistance. More current raises $V_B$ faster than $V_A$, so the amplifier must
*raise* PB (cutting current) when $V_B - V_A$ grows. On a PMOS-input OTA, the input on the diode side of
the load is the non-inverting one, so B goes there.

### Surprise 1: it worked first time, and it was wrong

The first run gave VREF = 1.228 V with only a 3.6 mV bow from −40 to 125 °C, which was suspiciously good.
I printed $\lvert V_{DS} \rvert$ and $V_{DSAT}$ for every device, and the amplifier's tail transistor had
45 mV across it against 230 mV needed. It was deep in triode. The circuit still regulated because the loop
has enough gain to hide it at one operating point, but a triode tail means poor rejection of supply and
common-mode changes. The number would not survive a supply sweep.

Adding up the stack from the inputs to VDD shows why:

$$
V_{CM} + \lvert V_{GS,in} \rvert + V_{DSAT,tail} \approx 0.72 + 1.04 + 0.23 \approx 2.0\ \text{V}
$$

The inputs sit a $V_{BE}$ above ground, and a standard sky130 PMOS has $\lvert V_{TH} \rvert$ ≈ 1.03 V at
this length. That's more than the 1.8 V available.

**Fix:** switch the input pair to `pfet_01v8_lvt` ($\lvert V_{TH} \rvert$ ≈ 0.56 V). The tail moved to 0.42 V
across it against 0.22 V needed, and every device passed the check.

### Surprise 2: a systematic offset

With the LVT pair, $V_A - V_B$ was 3.2 mV. That offset is amplified by about $1 + R_2/R_1$ on the way to VREF,
so it moved VREF by tens of millivolts. It was *systematic*, not random. The amplifier's output (PB, set by the mirror's $V_{SG}$)
sat at 0.54 V, while the diode side of its NMOS load sat at 0.62 V. Unequal drain voltages on a mirror load
mean unequal currents, which the loop cancels by tilting the inputs.

**Fix (partial):** longer amplifier devices for more gain. This only reduced the offset to 2.0 mV. The real fix came later (surprise 5).

### R2 tuning

With the circuit stable, I swept R2's length: 19.4 µm gave 1.2005 V at 27 °C. Each 0.1 µm moved VREF by about 2.5 mV,
so R2 is a sensitive trim. Note this for layout.

## Phase 2: drawing the schematic

I generated the xschem schematic from a small script, so every wire ends exactly on a pin, then checked it two ways:

- **Connectivity:** I netlisted it with xschem and compared the netlist to my hand version.
- **Behaviour:** I simulated that netlist directly.

### Surprise 3: the schematic gave a different answer from the hand netlist

The same circuit gave 1.2274 V from the xschem netlist and 1.2005 V from my hand netlist, 27 mV apart.
The netlists differed in three ways, so I tested each one separately:

| Variant | VREF |
|---|---|
| xschem netlist as drawn | 1.2274 V |
| …with the hand netlist's model library | **1.2005 V** |
| …with the PNP's duplicated `m=8 mult=8` reduced to `m=8` | 1.2274 V (no change) |
| …with the transistor area/perimeter parameters removed | 1.2274 V (no change) |

The cause was the model library. xschem's sky130 setup points at `libs.tech/combined`, while I had sized
against `libs.tech/ngspice`. The two give a different VREF for the same circuit.
**Fix:** use `combined` everywhere, because it's what anyone opening the schematic will simulate. Then retune R2 to 18.35 µm.

Two smaller things went wrong along the way:

- **Unevaluated parameters.** My script escaped the quotes in xschem's `expr(...)` geometry parameters, so the netlist received them as literal text.
  I switched to plain numbers.
- **An unnamed net.** The new loop-break net appeared as `net3`, because a wire's name attribute isn't a net name in xschem: only a label is.
  I added a `PBO` label.

There were also two scripting mistakes of my own:

- **Nothing measured.** The supply source's `savecurrent` option wrote `.save i(vsup)`, and once there's any `.save`, ngspice keeps *only* those vectors, so VREF wasn't saved.
  Adding `save all` fixed it.
- **The same R2 four times.** My R2 sweep loop edited a file that an earlier iteration had already changed, so it simulated the same R2 four times and printed identical results.
  It now starts from the clean netlist each time.

## Phase 3: the full test suite

I wrote the decks for operating point, temperature, supply, PSRR, start-up, loop gain, noise, corners and Monte Carlo, all sharing one bench.

### Surprise 4: VREF = −3,000 V

The first temperature sweep reported VREF between −3,030 and −3,600 V. The operating point at 27 °C was fine.
The same circuit had swept correctly an hour earlier, from the xschem testbench netlist.
I tested the differences one at a time: the PWL source, `save all`, `.temp`, and the order of `.lib` and `.include`. None of them mattered.

The answer is that a self-biased circuit has several DC solutions. A DC sweep's first point is solved from
scratch, and which solution it converges to depends on the solver's starting guesses, which depend on node ordering.
−3 kV is not physical. It's a numerical solution of the model equations that no real circuit could reach.

**Fix:** `.nodeset v(VREF)=1.2` in the bench, as an initial guess. That's only a hint to the solver, so I made
sure the start-up and Monte Carlo tests don't depend on it: they run in the time domain from VDD = 0.

### Surprise 5: poor supply rejection

The suite's numbers:

- **PSRR:** −19.6 dB.
- **Line regulation:** 107 mV/V.
- **Minimum supply:** VREF only reached its value above 1.71 V.

That's far worse than the 27 °C saturation check suggested.

There were two causes, both about what the amplifier is referenced to:

- **Tail headroom tracked VDD.** With PMOS inputs, the tail node sits at $V_B + \lvert V_{GS,in} \rvert$ ≈ 1.39 V,
  fixed by the PNPs. Every millivolt of supply droop came out of the tail's 0.42 V. At 1.62 V it was back in triode.
- **The output was referenced to the wrong rail.** An NMOS load references the amplifier output to ground, but
  the mirror gates it drives must sit a fixed $V_{SG}$ below VDD. So every supply change had to be corrected by the loop, and the loop only had about 32 dB of gain.

**Fix:** flip the amplifier to an NMOS-LVT input pair with a PMOS mirror load, plus a bias branch (M12, M13)
to set the tail current.

- **Tail:** the tail node is now $V_{CM} - V_{GS,in}$ ≈ 0.21 V, set by the PNPs, not by VDD.
- **Output:** the PMOS load references the output to VDD, so PB follows the supply on its own.
- **Offset:** I sized the load at the same current density as the mirror, so its $V_{SG}$ matches and the drains sit where the mirror gates need them.
  That also fixed surprise 2. The offset fell from 2 mV to 0.29 mV.

Results: PSRR −40 dB, line regulation 14 mV/V, minimum supply 1.52 V.

There was a side effect. Removing the 2 mV offset changed VREF too, because the old offset had been acting as a hidden trim on R2.
The temperature curve's peak moved from about 55 °C to −3 °C, so R2 needed retuning again.

### Surprise 6: loop gain that rose with frequency

The stability test injects a small AC voltage at Vloop, a 0 V source between the amplifier output (PBO) and the mirror gates (PB),
and computes the loop gain $T = -V_{PBO}/V_{PB}$. The result was flat at 32 dB, then *rose* to +49 dB above 10 MHz. Real loop gain doesn't do that.

Voltage injection is only accurate when the impedance looking into the injection point on one side
is much larger than on the other. I had put the compensation cap C1 on the PB side. At high frequency the cap shorts PB to VDD,
so $V_{PB} \to 0$ and the ratio blows up.

**Fix:** move C1 to the PBO side of the probe. In normal operation the circuit is identical, because Vloop is 0 V,
but now the PB side sees only gate capacitance and stays high-impedance.

### Surprise 7: phase margin of −87°

After that fix the test reported a phase margin of −87°, which would mean an oscillator. The start-up transient showed a clean,
unringing settle, so the measurement was suspect, not the circuit. My deck was printing the loop phase at crossover,
not the margin. The margin is 180° plus that phase, which gives +93°.

### Surprise 8: a fatal error at the ss corner

At the slow corner ngspice stopped with `Fatal: Pclm = -0.00102 is not positive` for M9, the start-up pull-up.
I had made M9 20 µm long to keep its current tiny. That length falls in a model bin whose parameters go out of range at ss.
**Fix:** L = 8 µm, which is still a weak pull-up at about 1.4 µA.

### Surprise 9: three broken Monte Carlo attempts

- **Attempt 1:** a mean of −26 V. Vectors created with `let` live in the current plot. Each `op` creates a new plot,
  so my results array was being written into plots that the next iteration couldn't see.
- **Attempt 2:** 162,000 lines of output for 200 samples. Writing a measured value with `wrdata` writes it against the whole time axis.
  **Fix:** `print vfinal >> out/mc.txt`, one line per sample.
- **Attempt 3 (operating point per sample):** 196 sensible values and four outliers, three near 0.68 V and one at 2.86 V, above the supply.
  These were the multiple-solution problem from surprise 4 again, now with random mismatch shifting the solver's path.

**Fix:** each sample now powers up with the same VDD ramp as the start-up test and is read after 40 µs. That's what a real chip does,
and it reports a sample that fails to start rather than hiding it. All 200 started.

### Surprise 10: mismatch much larger than drift

σ was 32 to 35 mV. That's 2 to 3 % of VREF, and many times larger than the whole temperature drift. Offsets in the amplifier and the PMOS mirror reach VREF
multiplied by about $1 + R_2/R_1$ ≈ 10.

**Fix:** quadruple every matched device's area at the same W/L, for example mirrors from 4/2 to 8/4 µm and inputs from 8/2 to 16/4 µm.
Each $V_{GS}$ and $V_{DSAT}$ stays almost the same, so the headroom work so far still held.
σ dropped to about 30 mV, and the longer channels raised the loop gain from 32 to 37 dB, PSRR to −46 dB, and offset to 0.10 mV.

### Surprise 11: 1.200 V versus a flat curve

Sweeping R2 showed a trade-off I hadn't planned for:

- **VREF at exactly 1.200 V** puts the temperature curve's peak near −35 °C and gives 77 ppm/°C.
- **Centring the peak** gives 30 to 36 ppm/°C, but at 1.22 to 1.23 V.

A first-order bandgap's flattest output voltage is set by the process, not chosen.
I kept the 1.2 V target, set R2 to 17.7 µm, and put the trade-off table on the main page.

## Phase 4: the saturation sweep and the hot corner

### Surprise 12: triode at 125 °C

When documenting the tuning, I turned the saturation check into a proper test and ran it at −40, 27 and 125 °C, not just 27 °C.
At 125 °C the tail M4 had a margin of **−61 mV**, in triode again. When hot:

- **$V_{BE}$ falls** to about 0.5 V, taking the input common-mode with it.
- **The input pair's $V_{GS}$ falls** less, so the tail node dropped to 0.11 V.
- **M4's $V_{DSAT}$ grows** to 0.18 V, because carrier mobility falls with temperature.

My first idea was to widen the tail and its bias diode. 3× wider gave −7 mV, and 5× wider only +10 mV. $V_{DSAT}$ has a floor near 0.1 V
in moderate inversion, so the tail node itself had to come up. I prototyped two changes together in the netlist before touching the schematic:

- **Level-shift resistors.** RCA and RCB sit above A and B and carry equal currents, so they lift both amplifier inputs by
  the same $I \cdot R_C$. The amplifier forces $V_{AH} = V_{BH}$, which still means $V_A = V_B$, so the bandgap equation is untouched.
- **LVT PMOS mirror and load.** The lift has to come from somewhere. Switching M1 to M3, M12, M7 and M8 to `pfet_01v8_lvt`
  cut their $V_{SG}$ from 1.26 to about 0.75 V, raising PB from 0.54 to 1.05 V and giving the input pair's drains room to rise.

Every analog device then kept at least 0.1 V of margin across −40 to 125 °C at both 1.8 and 1.62 V.

A small tooling surprise came with it. After switching the mirrors to LVT, the saturation check printed nothing for them. It had the model names
hard-coded, so `pfet_01v8` parameters didn't exist on `pfet_01v8_lvt` devices. **Fix:** `make_sat.py` now generates the deck from the netlist every run.

### Surprise 13: the fix "broke" everything

The prototype looked clean, but when I applied the same change to the schematic, the suite returned nonsense:

- **Operating point:** VREF = 0.68 V, with the supply apparently *delivering* 4.4 mW.
- **Corners:** ss, ff and fs swept up to about 1.7 V.
- **PSRR:** −16 dB.
- **Phase margin:** −33°.

I diffed the schematic's netlist against the prototype device by device, and they matched exactly. So the circuit was right,
and the solver was lost: the LVT mirror had moved PB from 0.54 to 1.05 V, far from where a `.nodeset` on VREF alone pointed it.

The 1.7 V state worried me, because it could be a real second stable state, a latch. That would be a design bug, not a simulator artifact. I tested it two ways:

- **Power-up from 0 V at every corner:** all five settled at 1.197 to 1.201 V.
- **A transient started from the 1.7 V state at ss:** it fell back to 1.1996 V.

So it was an unstable equilibrium. The DC solver can land on it, but a real circuit can't stay there.

**Fixes:**

- **Fuller nodeset** (VREF, PB, A and B), which repaired the operating point.
- **The temperature sweep runs outward from 27 °C,** once up to 125 °C and once down to −40 °C, so every point continues from the guided
  solution instead of starting cold at −40 °C. All five corners then swept cleanly.

### Surprise 14: mismatch tripled

The 200-run Monte Carlo gave σ = 109 mV, with samples from 1.0 to 1.55 V. These weren't start-up failures, but a genuinely broad spread.
I ran 60-sample variants to find which change was responsible:

| Variant | σ |
|---|---|
| LVT PMOS + level-shift resistors (as designed) | 96 mV |
| Same, but the amplifier senses A and B directly (resistors out of the offset path) | **29 mV** |
| Standard PMOS + level-shift resistors | 61 mV |

The resistors were the cause. Any difference between RCA and RCB adds $I \cdot \Delta R_C$ directly to the amplifier offset,
which is then multiplied by 10. The narrow 0.35 × 9 µm resistors I'd used match poorly.

**Fix:** use the widest poly resistor, `res_xhigh_po_2p85`, 2.85 × 60 µm for the same ~42 kΩ, about 54 times the area.
σ came down to 45 to 50 mV over three 200-run sets. That's still worse than the 30 mV before the hot-corner fix, so this is a
real trade-off between working at 125 °C and accuracy. Layout or trim has to close the gap.

Two loose ends remained after that:

- **VREF had moved** to 1.198 V, so I trimmed R2 to 17.77 µm.
- **A small kink at 27 °C** appeared in the temperature plot (surprise 15), where the two half-sweeps join, about 0.2 mV high.
  ngspice's default `reltol` is 0.1 %, which is 1.2 mV on VREF, so the first few points of each sweep weren't fully converged.
  **Fix:** `.option reltol=1e-5`, after which the curve is smooth.

### One more correction: my own explanation

On the main page I first wrote that the bias current was below the ideal $V_T \ln 8 / R_1$ because of low PNP gain.
Before committing, I measured instead:

- **Across R1:** 54.5 mV, matching $V_T \ln 8$ = 53.7 mV. The theory holds.
- **R1 itself:** the PDK model gives 14.5 kΩ in simulation, not the 11.4 kΩ that xschem's symbol estimates from sheet resistance.

That accounts for the 3.75 µA current. I corrected the page.

## Tooling problems

These didn't change the circuit, but each one cost a detour:

| Problem | What happened | Workaround |
|---|---|---|
| Schematic SVG export | xschem's GUI start hung intermittently | Retry loop with a timeout |
| Schematic readability | The amplifier block's labels overlapped | Spread the block out; re-simulated to confirm identical results |
| Noise measurement | `meas` doesn't work on noise analyses in ngspice | Spot noise read from the data in `plot.py` |
| LVS netlist | xschem's LVS mode flag didn't take effect; the top level was a commented-out `.subckt` | Post-process the netlist: un-comment the subckt, strip simulation-only parameters, short 0 V probes |
| Passing LVS demo | The PDK's KLayout transistor generators need `gdsfactory`; magic crashed in headless mode | Deferred to the bandgap layout |
| Site build | No Node in WSL | Installed Node 22 with nvm |

## Lessons

- **Check saturation at every corner, not one point.** Both triode problems produced a plausible VREF.
  The second only showed up at 125 °C, after the design had passed everything else.
- **Each fix has a cost somewhere else.** Removing an offset moved the temperature curve. Raising the tail's headroom
  cost mismatch. Getting VREF to exactly 1.2 V cost flatness.
- **Suspect the solver before the circuit, then prove which one it is.** A −3 kV result, a 2.86 V Monte Carlo sample and a 1.7 V corner were all DC-solver artifacts.
  I only trusted that after a transient from the suspicious state showed the real circuit wouldn't stay there.
- **Isolate by changing one thing at a time.** The model-library difference and the mismatch cause were both found with small tables of variants, not by reasoning.
